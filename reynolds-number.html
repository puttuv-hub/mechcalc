Formula

Re = ρVD / μ

Where:
Re = Reynolds number
ρ = Fluid density
V = Flow velocity
D = Characteristic length
μ = Dynamic viscosity

Flow Classification

For internal flow through a circular pipe, the commonly used approximate ranges are:

Re < 2300: Laminar

2300–4000: Transitional

Re > 4000: Turbulent

These limits are primarily for fully developed internal pipe flow and can differ for other geometries.

Understanding Reynolds Number

Reynolds number is a dimensionless quantity used to compare inertial effects with viscous effects in fluid flow.

It is widely used in pipe-flow analysis, pumps, turbines, heat exchangers, aircraft, vehicles and many other engineering applications.

  • Low Reynolds number: Viscous effects are relatively dominant.
  • High Reynolds number: Inertial effects are relatively dominant.
  • Reynolds number: Dimensionless.
  • Pipe-flow characteristic length: Usually the pipe diameter.
